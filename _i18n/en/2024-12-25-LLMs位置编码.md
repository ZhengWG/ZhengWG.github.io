## Preface

In large language models (`LLMs`, `Large Language Models`), the Transformer itself has no built-in sense of sequence, so positional encoding is introduced to help the model capture **positional relationships** and **order** in the input sequence.

## Absolute Positional Encoding

Absolute positional encoding encodes each position directly as a unique fixed vector. Common absolute positional encodings include:

1. Learned positional encoding

   Each position corresponds to a trainable vector, and the model learns its semantics, as in a learned `Posional Embedding`.

2. Fixed sinusoidal positional encoding (Sinusoidal PE)

   Proposed by `Google` in [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03762). Sine and cosine functions generate the position vector. Periodicity and a decreasing frequency let the model extend naturally to lengths it has not seen:

   $$\text{PE}{(pos, 2i)} = \sin(\frac {pos} {10000^{2i/d}})$$

   $$\text{PE}{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos(\frac {pos} {10000^{2i/d}})$$

The problem with absolute positional encoding is:

It does not extrapolate. Absolute positional encoding cannot express relative position, so once the input length exceeds the maximum length seen in training, the results drop sharply. A `Sinusoidal` encoding can learn relative position, and it has long-range decay, but that relative-position information is not reflected after the $QK^T$ inner product.

## RoPE

Ideally, a good positional encoding should meet the following conditions:

1. Each position produces a unique encoding
2. It extrapolates well: relative-position information is still present after the $QK^T$ inner product
3. The relative distance between any two positions should be consistent across sentences of different lengths

The derivation in [ROPE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09864) is as follows.

Assume the function that computes $q/k/v$ is $f$. It computes $q/k/v$ from the word-embedding vector $x$ and, at the same time, brings in the positional encoding:

$$q_m=f_q(x_m, m)$$

$$k_n=f_k(x_n, n)$$

$$v_n=f_v(x_n, n)$$

Here $m/n$ denotes position. Let $g$ denote the inner product of $q/k$:

$$<f_q(x_m,m),f_k(x_n,n)>=g(x_m,x_n,m-n)$$

The input of $g$ is the relative position $m-n$. A positional encoding has to be found that satisfies the equality above. Euler's formula can be brought in:

$$e^{ix}=\text{cos}x+i\text{sin}x$$

That is:

$$f_q(x_m, m) = (W_qx_m)e^{imθ}$$

$$f_k(x_n, n) = (W_kx_n)e^{inθ}$$

$$g(x_m, x_n, m − n) = \text{Re}[(W_qx_m)(W_kx_n) ∗ e^{i(m−n)θ}]$$

Expanding a single $f$ into adjacent two-dimensional vectors gives:

$$q_m=\begin{pmatrix} q_m^{(1)} \\ q_m^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} W_q^{(11)} & W_q^{(12)} \\ W_q^{(21)} &W_q^{(22)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x_m^{(1)} \\ x_m^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}$$

Written in complex form:

$$q_m=[q_m^{(1)},q_m^{2}]=q_m^{(1)}+iq_m^{(2)}$$

which gives:

$$q_me^{im\theta}=(q_m^{(1)}+iq_m^{(2)})*(\text{cos}(m\theta)+i\text{sin}(m\theta))=(q_m^{(1)}\text{cos}(m\theta)-q_m^{(2)}\text{sin}(m\theta))+i(q_m^{(2)}\text{cos}(m\theta)+q_m^{(1)}\text{sin}(m\theta))$$

The expression above is exactly the RoPE rotation matrix:

$$f_q(x_m,m)=\begin{pmatrix} \text{cos}m\theta & -\text{sin}m\theta \\ \text{sin}m\theta & \text{cos}m\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} q_m^{(1)} \\ q_m^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}=R^d_{\Theta,m}\begin{pmatrix} q_m^{(1)} \\ q_m^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}$$

Extending the 2-dimensional matrix to more dimensions, the rotation matrix is:

$$R^d_{\Theta,m}=\begin{pmatrix} \text{cos}m\theta_0 & -\text{sin}m\theta_0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \text{sin}m\theta_0 & \text{cos}m\theta_0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \text{cos}m\theta_1 & -\text{sin}m\theta_1 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \text{sin}m\theta_1 & \text{cos}m\theta_1 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \text{cos}m\theta_{d/2-1} & -\text{sin}m\theta_{d/2-1} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \text{sin}m\theta_{d/2-1} & \text{cos}m\theta_{d/2-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} q_m^{(1)} \\ q_m^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}$$

$$\Theta=\{\theta_i=1000^{-2(i-1)/d},i\in[0,1,\cdots,d/2] \}$$

Because $R^d_{\Theta,m}$ is sparse, multiplying by the matrix directly is inefficient. The following implementation can be used instead:

$$R^d_{\Theta,m}x=\begin{pmatrix}x_0 \\ x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ \vdots \\ x_{d-2} \\ x_{d-1}\end{pmatrix} \otimes \begin{pmatrix}\text{cos}m\theta_0 \\ \text{cos}m\theta_0 \\ \text{cos}m\theta_1 \\ \text{cos}m\theta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \text{cos}m\theta_{d/2-1} \\ \text{cos}m\theta_{d/2-1}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}-x_1 \\ x_0 \\ -x_3 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ -x_{d-1} \\ x_{d-2}\end{pmatrix} \otimes \begin{pmatrix}\text{sin}m\theta_0 \\ \text{sin}m\theta_0 \\ \text{sin}m\theta_1 \\ \text{sin}m\theta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \text{sin}m\theta_{d/2-1} \\ \text{sin}m\theta_{d/2-1}\end{pmatrix}$$

The computation of RoPE is therefore as follows.

For the word-embedding vectors of a token sequence, first compute the query and key vectors, then compute the corresponding rotary positional encoding, then apply the rotation to query and key `in pairs`, and finally compute the inner product.

## ALiBi

[ALiBi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.12409) (`Attention with Linear Biases`) was published at `ICLR2022`. The institutions include. The core idea of `ALiBi` is:

> Attention with Linear Biases, biases query-key attention scores with a penalty that is proportional to their distance.

`AliBi` writes a linear bias that depends on relative position directly onto the result of $QK^T$:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K)_{i,j} = \frac{Q_i K_j^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + b(i, j)$$

where $b(i,j)$ is the linear bias:

$$b(i,j)=-|i-j| \cdot m$$

Here $i,j$ are the sequence-position indices of the $query/key$, and $m$ is a scale factor. It is a `head-specific slope`, corresponding to $2^{\frac {-8} {n}}$. For 8 heads, $m=\frac{1}{2^1},\frac{1}{2^2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2^8}$.
